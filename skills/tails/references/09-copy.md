# Copy

Loaded whenever a human will read the words. Generic copy and generic layout are the same
defect and they are usually written by the same shortcut.

## The hard floor: nothing invented

**No claim, number, review, award, certification, year of founding, customer count or
local detail that is not in a source you can point at.**

This is not a stylistic preference. On a page built for a real business it is the
difference between a proposal and a forgery. If the information is missing:

1. Ask for it.
2. If it cannot be obtained, **name the gap in the handoff** — "the testimonials section
   is empty because there are no testimonials to use".
3. If a placeholder is unavoidable, make it **visibly** placeholder. Never plausible
   filler; plausible filler ships by accident.

Same rule for images: a photograph of a place that is not the place is a lie with better
production values.

## Say what happens

The single cheapest improvement to almost any page: **make every action name its
outcome.**

| Generic | Says what happens |
|---|---|
| Get Started | See the showtimes |
| Learn more | What a rewire costs |
| Submit | Send the booking request |
| Discover | Read the three questions we ask first |

An action keeps the same name through the whole flow. The button that says *Publish*
produces a message that says *Published*.

## Write from the reader's side

- **Name things as the reader recognises them**, never as the system is built. A person
  manages notifications, not webhook configuration.
- **Describe what it does**, do not sell it. Specific beats clever, every time.
- **Active voice**, sentence case, no filler.
- **Each element does one job.** A label labels. An example demonstrates. Nothing does
  double duty quietly.

## Failure and emptiness are directions, not moods

An error says what happened and what to do about it, in the interface's voice. It does
not apologise and it is never vague. An empty screen is an invitation to act, not a
shrug.

## Show, do not assert

Expertise shows through **specific observation**, not adjectives. "We measure where the
signal drops before touching the router" carries more authority than "expert technicians"
because it could only be written by someone who does the work.

Name limits and edge cases where they are relevant. Saying what you do *not* do is one of
the strongest trust signals available and costs nothing.

## The banned register

These phrases are not wrong, they are **empty**, and their presence marks text as
unwritten:

> seamless · cutting-edge · innovative · holistic · tailored solutions · in today's
> digital world · it is important to note · unlock · elevate · revolutionary · next-gen ·
> best-in-class · we take care of everything · 360-degree · state of the art

Also structural: an opening paragraph that says what the page will say; a closing
paragraph that repeats it; a "not only X but also Y" construction; perfectly symmetrical
sentence pairs; and em-dashes used as a rhythm device rather than punctuation.

## The test

Read it and ask: **could this text appear verbatim on a competitor's site?**

If yes, it says nothing about this business, and the reader — who is comparing three
sites in three tabs — has no reason to pick this one.
